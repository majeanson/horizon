## 1. Federal: GST/HST credit is now the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (CGEB) — **engine gap + naming**

Official: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-groceries-essentials-benefit/how-much/payment-amounts.html
(page dated 2026-06-08) and `.../how-much.html` (2026-07-15).

- « The GST/HST credit has been renamed the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (CGEB) as of July 2026 » and
  « In July 2026, there was a 25% increase to the benefit amount which will remain for 5 years from 2026 to 2031 ».
- Base year 2025 (paid July 2026 – June 2027): individual 445 $ · spouse 445 $ · each child under 19 234 $ · first
  child of a single parent 445 $ · additional amount for a single individual 234 $ · phase-in threshold for that
  additional amount 11 564 $ · **phase-out threshold 46 432 $**.

- Prior year (2024 base): 349 / 349 / 184 / 349 / 184 / 11 337 / 45 521.

## 2. Québec solidarity tax credit — **engine gap**

Official (ceilings, rule): Revenu Québec « Crédit d'impôt pour solidarité – Aide par ligne »,
https://www.revenuquebec.ca/fr/citoyens/declaration-de-revenus/produire-votre-declaration-de-revenus/comment-remplir-votre-declaration-de-revenus/aide-par-ligne/credit-dimpot-pour-solidarite/
Payment July 2026 – June 2027 uses the 31 December 2025 situation. Family income = line 275 (+ spouse's).
Maximum family income: couple 70 395 $ + 2 634 $ per dependent child; single parent 64 545 $ + 2 634 $ per
child; single 64 545 $. Payment frequency: ≥ 800 $ monthly, 241–799 $ quarterly, ≤ 240 $ once in July.

Official (rule): Finance Québec, « Crédit d'impôt remboursable pour la solidarité » (Dépenses fiscales 2025),
https://www.budget.finances.gouv.qc.ca/ — reduction « selon un taux de 6 % (3 % si le particulier ne bénéficie que
d'une seule des trois composantes) pour chaque dollar … qui excède le seuil de réduction »; the result cannot be
below what the TVQ component alone would give. Statute: Loi sur les impôts, art. 1029.8.116.16.

Secondary (amounts, 2026-27), CFFP Université de Sherbrooke
https://cffp.recherche.usherbrooke.ca/outils-ressources/guide-mesures-fiscales/credit-impot-solidarite/ :
TVQ base 363 $ · spouse 363 $ · living alone 172 $ · housing: single/lone parent 746 $, couple 906 $, child
supplement 158 $ · reduction threshold **43 195 $** · 6 % / 3 %. Max for a single person 1 281 $.

**Discrepancy to settle before using.** With a 43 195 $ threshold, 6 % and a 1 281 $ maximum the credit hits the
TVQ-only floor and the official 64 545 $ ceiling can only be reproduced if the floor itself is reduced at 3 %
from a different threshold. 43 195 + 535 / 0,03 = 61 028 $, not 64 545 $. So one of: a different threshold for
the TVQ-only leg, a different TVQ-only maximum, or the ceiling table uses another rule. The Annexe D (TP-1.D) PDF
for 2025 settles it; Revenu Québec's component pages print no dollar amounts in text (the English and French
pages were read; amounts sit in the estimator).

Also in force: a **one-time « versement spécial pour l'épicerie et l'énergie »** paid 4–12 June 2026 to people
eligible for the 2025-26 solidarity period: 100 $ single, 150 $ couple, +50 $ per household with minor children.
One-off, not to be projected.

## 8. Separation: QPP credit splitting and the FHSA

- Retraite Québec « Le partage des gains entre conjoints »
  (https://www.retraitequebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/citoyens/retraite-et-planification/retraite/retraite-deux/vous-vous-separez/partage-gains-entre-conjoints):
  on divorce / annulment / dissolution decided in Québec, the earnings credited to the QPP during the marriage are
  added and split equally, automatically, unless expressly waived (waiving the family patrimony is not waiving this).
  De facto couples: no automatic split, joint request within 4 years of separation; period = whole calendar years from
  1 January of the year cohabitation began to 31 December of the year before it ended. Nobody is paid until entitled to a
  QPP pension; an existing pension is raised or cut by the split. Retraite Québec will simulate before/after for free.
  Not modelled in the engine (no grep hit): the app takes each person's relevé as given.
- CRA FHSA « Participating in your FHSAs »
  (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account/contributing-your-fhsa.html):
  participation room 8 000 $ in the year the first account opens; later years 8 000 $ plus carry-forward, capped by
  40 000 $ lifetime minus contributions and RRSP transfers; income earned does not use room. Eligibility, the 15-year
  limit and the age limit sit on other CRA pages not read here. Relevant only to the youngest households.

## 13. Audit of the work so far (2026-10-23, after the CGEB, the glossary popup and the two Québec credits)

Done and verified against official pages: CGEB (30 rows of the CRA chart), senior assistance credit (three ceilings), solidarity credit (three ceilings + two maxima).
Cut corners and things overlooked, each with where to fix it:
1. **The « coût d'un dollar de plus » (marginalRate.ts) ignores the credits.** It counts tax, the OAS recovery and the GIS lost, all in the SAME year. The credits read LAST year's income, so a dollar drawn this year
   costs 5,4 % (senior assistance, 70+), 6 % (solidarity, up to about 64 500 $ alone / 70 400 $ as a couple) and 5 % (groceries benefit, above 46 432 $) NEXT year. For a retiree between about 28 000 $ and 65 000 $ of
   income that is up to 16 points the weather strip does not show. Fix: add a `credits` term (difference of the three credit functions at income and income + delta) to MarginalRate and to the strip's copy.
2. **The solidarity credit's housing component is applied to everyone.** A public CHSLD, a low-rental housing unit and a room in a family home are excluded (Annexe D, TP-1.D.D p. 2). The engine does not model where a
   person lives, only what care costs: a household in a CHSLD overstates the credit by 746 $ to 906 $ a year. Documented in qcCredits.ts; the care model could switch it off.
3. **Deferring the pensions to 70 is worth less than the app used to say** for a household the credits carry: two examples (`newcomer`, `early`) now end with 18 000 $ and 21 000 $ LESS from `pensions70`. The wording
   was read and is right; the test lists them (whatIfCopy.test.ts). This is a real finding worth a sentence on Décider: « les crédits du Québec fondent à mesure que la rente monte ».
4. **Nothing on screen says what the credits are.** They appear as a bar (« Crédits et allocations ») and a column of the bridge table, never named one by one; the Glossaire has ACEBE only. To add: a glossary term for each
   credit, and the year readout (« Une année en détail ») should list the parts, not one sum.
5. **The year table (Résultats) has no credits column**; the child benefits never had one either. « Nothing hidden » (STATE phase 11) argues for one.
6. **Not run locally:** Playwright (a11y colour contrast of the new sage tier beside two other sage tiers, the chart legend, the print views). CI's e2e will be the first to see them.
7. **Still open from the first pass:** the drug-plan premium (needs the Annexe K bands), the home-support credit (needs the cost of home services in the model), the FHSA, a survival view, a care-cost growth assumption.
8. **A fixture was changed to keep its story** (`couple62` spending 48 000 $ to 50 000 $, `downsizer` 80 000 $ to 81 000 $): the credits carry a modest plan, and these examples exist to show a plan that runs short and a sale that is
   what makes 58 work. Each change is commented in golden/examples.ts.
9. **Answers that move with the credits:** modest 63 → 62 (after the CGEB: 64 → 63), behind 70 → 68, newcomer 63 → 61, early 46 → 45, family 60 → 59, planner's « without a child » 55 → 54.

## 14. Annexe K read (the drug-plan premium), official — and why it is not built yet

`research/sources/TP-1.D.K-2025-12-annexe-K-medicaments.pdf` (Revenu Québec, TP-1.D.K, tax year 2025; the file name pattern is TP-1.D.<letter> — Annexe D is TP-1.D.D). Marc's « I opened Annexe K » was this form.
Premium for the tax year 2025, per person with a full year of public coverage:
- Base = line 275 (both spouses) − 32 240 $ (couple) or 19 890 $ (single), − for children 4 220 $ / 8 120 $ (couple, one / several) or 12 350 $ / 16 570 $ (single parent); nothing to pay if it is ≤ 0.
- Base ≤ 5 000 $: 7,84 % of it (single, max 392 $) or 3,93 % (couple, max 196,50 $ each).
- 5 000 $ < base ≤ 14 669 $: single 392 $ + 11,76 % of the part over 5 000 $; couple, each, 196,50 $ + 5,89 % over 5 000 $ — which reaches **766 $ exactly at 14 669 $** (the secondary CFFP table was right for a couple, wrong for a single person, whose 766 $ cap bites at 8 180 $).
- Base > 14 669 $: 766 $. Then reduced for months without a premium (private plan, GIS-free months, care in a CHSLD, under 18 …) and capped at the year's maximum: **755 $** for 2025 (62,00 $ × 6 + 63,83 $ × 6), 789 $ from July 2026 (RAMQ).
- No premium either for a 65+ on the GIS who got free drugs all year (codes 27–31, 33, 35: net federal supplements over 12 467 $ single, 7 468 $ / 6 892 $ / 11 534 $ couples).
Why it is not in the engine: it needs to know whether each person is on the PUBLIC plan or a private group plan (most people under 65 with an employer are on the private one; 65+ may keep it). The profile has no such question, so a default would be a guess in one direction or the other; adding the question is a schema change (version bump, migration, a field in Profil). **Product decision for Marc:** add « Assurance médicaments » (public / privée / les deux) on Profil, then model the premium as a payment made with the return, and the 94 % GIS line as the exemption.
